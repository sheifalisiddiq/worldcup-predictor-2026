# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Football fans worldwide — casual supporters to stat-obsessed analysts — who want to predict every match of FIFA World Cup 2026 and compete against friends and the globe. They use the app before, during, and after matches on mobile phones in the moment: in pubs, living rooms, half-watching pre-game coverage. The job to be done: lock in a scoreline prediction before kickoff, feel the stakes, check the table. Emotion is primary; precision is the measure.

## Product Purpose

Predict all 104 World Cup 2026 matches. Earn points for correct results and exact scores. Climb a real-time global leaderboard. The app exists to channel the universal football obsession into a structured, shareable, competitive ritual. Success looks like a player who checks it compulsively from the group stage to the final.

## Brand Personality

Loud. Iconic. Alive.

Emotional goals: anticipation before kickoff, triumph when an exact score lands, the collective charge of a tournament that only comes every four years. Not a utility — a rally point.

## Anti-references

- Generic sports-app blue-and-white (ESPN, Sky Sports template feel)
- Soft-shadow card grids with pastel accent chips
- "AI made this in 3 minutes" vibes: rounded corners, gradient buttons, Inter everywhere
- Fantasy football spreadsheet aesthetics
- Anything that would look at home inside a bland SaaS dashboard

## Design Principles

1. **The stadium, not the spreadsheet.** Every screen should feel like you're inside the tournament, not managing data about it. Color and type carry the noise of 100,000 fans.
2. **Brand IS content.** The FIFA WC 2026 visual identity — the six-color stripe, the FWC2026 condensed type, the navy-sunburst stage presence — is not decoration. It is the experience.
3. **Hard edges, high stakes.** Neo-brutalist borders and offset shadows communicate permanence and decisiveness. A prediction locked in must FEEL locked in.
4. **All colors simultaneously.** The WC2026 palette has six primary colors. Use all of them, not one accent. Each stage gets its own identity. The leaderboard podium burns gold, blue, and copper.
5. **Emotion before information.** The landing page goal is not to explain the product — it is to make someone feel the World Cup before they read a word. Type first. Hierarchy second. Fine print last.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. High-contrast mandatory (the dark-navy default exceeds AA). Reduced-motion must not break any functionality — animations are enhancement only. Color is never the sole differentiator for state (locked/unlocked, points/no points).
